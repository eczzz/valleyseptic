Spring in Abbotsford, BC, brings blooming gardens and longer days—but it also brings challenges for septic systems.
With melting snow, increased rainfall, and higher water usage, spring is a critical time to ensure your septic system is functioning properly.
Neglecting maintenance can lead to backups, foul odors, and costly repairs.

At Valley Septic and Plumbing, we understand the unique needs of homeowners in the Fraser Valley.
Here’s a guide to help you navigate the top three septic issues that often arise in spring and how to prevent them.

1. Overloaded Septic Tanks

Spring often means more water entering your septic system—from rain, snowmelt, and increased indoor usage.
This influx can overwhelm your tank, especially if it hasn’t been pumped recently.
An overloaded tank may lead to slow drains, sewage backups, and unpleasant odors in your home or yard.

Prevention Tips:

  • Schedule Regular Pumping: Ensure your tank is pumped every 3–5 years, or more frequently for larger households. Learn more about septic tank pumping in Abbotsford.
  • Monitor Water Usage: Spread out laundry and dishwasher loads to avoid sudden surges.
  • Install Water-Efficient Fixtures: Use low-flow toilets and showerheads to reduce water entering the system.

2. Saturated Drainfields

The drainfield disperses wastewater into the soil. However, spring rains can saturate the ground, hindering this process.
When the soil is too wet, it can’t absorb effluent effectively, leading to pooling water, slow drains, and potential system failure.

Signs of Trouble:

  • Soggy or spongy lawn areas, especially near the drainfield.
  • Unusual plant growth, such as lush, green patches.
  • Foul odors lingering around the yard.

Preventive Measures:

  • Redirect gutters and downspouts away from the drainfield.
  • Avoid parking or driving heavy equipment over the drainfield.
  • Schedule septic inspections regularly to catch problems early.

3. Alarm System Malfunctions

Modern septic systems often include alarms to alert homeowners of issues like high water levels or pump failures.
Spring’s moisture can impact these components, leading to false alarms or, worse, undetected malfunctions.

What to Watch For:

  • Frequent or unexplained alarms.
  • Alarms not activating during obvious system failures.

Action Steps:

  • Test alarms regularly to ensure functionality.
  • Inspect electrical components for corrosion or damage.
  • Consider professional help with septic alarm services if needed.

Seasonal Maintenance Tips

  • Limit garbage disposal use to reduce solid buildup.
  • Use septic-safe cleaning products to protect the system’s bacterial balance.
  • Keep a maintenance record for pumping, inspections, and repairs.
